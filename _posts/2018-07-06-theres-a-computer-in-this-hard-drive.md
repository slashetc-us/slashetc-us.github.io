<p>Throughout the history of personal computers, there are some unique form factors. The 3com Audrey was sold as a computing appliance, meant to sit on a kitchen counter, to display recipes or something. For some reason, Macs were cubes once, and it actually wasn&rsquo;t a bad machine. At one point, you were supposed to put a monitor&nbsp;<em>on top</em> of your computer.</p>
<p>A few years ago, [glitch] read about an interesting system from the early 80s. The SIIG S286 was designed by the same people that made SCSI cards and external hard drives, and it shows: this is a complete 286-based system stuffed into what was probably an external enclosure for a 5 1/4 drive at some point. After finding one of these bad boys on an auction site a few months ago, <a href="http://www.glitchwrks.com/2018/07/01/siig-s286" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">he finally got it working</a>. It&rsquo;s weird, but it can get on a network, and you can read Hackaday with it.</p>
<p><a href="https://hackadaycom.files.wordpress.com/2018/07/hackaday.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/hackaday.jpg?w=400&amp;h=300" alt="" width="400" height="300" /></a>The entire computer is stuffed into a case that&rsquo;s about 5&Prime; wide, 4&Prime; tall, and 10&Prime; long. There&rsquo;s a motherboard with built-in VGA, &lsquo;game port&rsquo;, and a printer port. There&rsquo;s a riser card for real 16-bit ISA cards, two serial ports, and a connector for a hard disk and floppy drive. Basically, it&rsquo;s an entire 286 system wrapped up in a tiny box.</p>
<p>After acquiring this machine, [glitch] took it apart and found the usual damage. The CMOS battery leaked, but not too bad. This was replaced with a hermetically sealed lithium thionyl chloride battery. These are non-rechargable, but a quick swipe of the soldering iron disable the motherboard&rsquo;s charging circuitry. The hard drive was replaced with a 128 MB Flash module, and an Ethernet card was installed.</p>
<p>With that, [glitch] has a complete system that can connect to the Internet. Of course, getting on the Internet with a 286 is a challenge, <a href="http://retro.hackaday.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">but we have a Hackaday Retro Edition</a>&nbsp;for just the occasion. The browser is Arache, with the mTCP package. That&rsquo;s about as low as you can go in Intel-land, and excellent proof that the computer will work for another 35 years or so.</p>
